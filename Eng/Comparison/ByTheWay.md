## By the way vs by the wayside
**by the wayside** is a part of the expression **to fall by the wayside** meaning _to be abandoned, neglected, or dropped along the way_. **go by the wayside** is a common informal variant, but a careful writer would prefer **fall**.  
**by the way** means _incidentally_, used to introduce a side remark. Originally it meant something noticed while travelling along the road, so the two expressions share a root metaphor but have drifted far apart. Quick test: if you can substitute "incidentally," it's **by the way**. If you can substitute "abandoned" or "left behind," it's **by the wayside**.  
Eg.   
A lot of marriages fall by the wayside because couples cannot talk to each other.   
By the way, how is John?    
Here, **by the way** takes a different meaning _by the manner in which_:    
I was enchanted by the way she smiled.   
I knew he was lying by the way he avoided my eyes.
